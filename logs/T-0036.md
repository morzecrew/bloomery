## Self-audit — 2026-09-10

Scope: the whole change — one new page, README, `guardrails.md`, the nav, one
gate, the RFC status and the changelog. Three findings, all in prose I wrote,
all fixed. Prose is the deliverable here, so §7 of the sweep — lies in prose —
is the pass that carries the weight, and §8's four questions are the oracle.

**1 · My gloss under §4 claimed more than §4 does.** The RFC's closed-world
statement says bloomery does not infer semantics heuristically and grows
capability by adding rules. I glossed that as "where a rule to justify an
operation does not exist, the operation is refused rather than allowed on the
grounds that nothing objected" — which describes the finished state RFC 0039 is
building toward, not today's. Some acceptances carry a positive derivation and
others are a documented check that found nothing wrong, and the second is
weaker: it rules out the failures somebody thought of. Both refuse rather than
guess, so neither answers wrongly in silence, but only the first can say why it
is safe. The page now says exactly that, in a note, rather than implying the
conversion is finished.

Worth naming as the shape rather than the instance: a verbatim quote from a
spec can be true while the sentence *underneath* it, written to explain it,
over-claims — and the quote lends the gloss its authority.

**2 · An example asserted more than the RFC's own conditional.** §3 says an
order-grain measure "may be safely answered by customer country **when**
bloomery can aggregate from Order to Country through grain-preserving
relationships". My gloss dropped the when and read as though the request always
plans. Restored, with a link to where the refusal is documented.

**3 · The D7 gate passed a sabotage twice before it bit.** First it read the
whole section, so dropping Cube from the boundary sentence still passed —
because the prose below mentions Cube while making a different point. Scoped to
the admonition, it still passed, because slicing to the next `##` heading
included that same prose. Cut at the block's own indentation it fails on every
one of the four targets dropped in turn, which is what was measured before this
was called done.

That is the finding, not the two false starts: a claim gate that greps a region
larger than the claim will pass on any word that happens to appear nearby, and
it looks identical to a gate that works.
