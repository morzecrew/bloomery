## D-111 — a proof is built on demand, never retained

```divergence
decision: RFC 0039 D7 — whether a proof is retained after acceptance or discarded once the obligation closes
grade: OPEN
class: discovery
at: 2026-09-05T15:58:00Z
attempt: 1
claim: the question does not arise in the shape the row expects. `can_roll_up` already constructs a proof and returns it, and nothing stores one on ProjectIR, so a compile pays nothing for proofs it does not ask for and `explain` obtains one by asking the same function the planner will. action: decided
proposal: D7 closes as "built on demand, never retained". A retention flag is not needed and would be the wrong shape: it would make the proof a property of the compile rather than the answer to a question, and the question is what `explain` and RFC 0040's planner both ask
```

## D-112 — rule IDs are append-only, and a split mints new ones

```divergence
decision: RFC 0039 D8 — what happens to a rule ID when a rule is split or subsumed
grade: OPEN
class: spec-gap
at: 2026-09-05T16:00:00Z
attempt: 1
claim: the row requires this decided before the first ID ships and gives no candidate; the repository already has the answer in two places it applies to identifiers that outlive their definitions — RFC decision rows are append-only and superseded rather than edited, and retired RFC numbers are never reused
evidence: `grep -n "append-only" .claude/skills/rfc-writer/SKILL.md` -> `- **The decision table is append-only.** A superseded row stays, marked superseded, naming the row that replaced it`
action: decided
proposal: D8 closes as "append-only, by the same discipline the decision tables use". A rule ID is never reused and never silently repointed; splitting a rule mints new IDs and leaves the old one in the registry marked superseded, naming its replacements. The registry is a module constant a test asserts against, so a removed ID fails rather than disappearing — which is the half a convention cannot enforce
```

## Findings — self-audit, 2026-09-05

Scope: the whole branch, 3 commits, 7 files, +1205/−5 — source, tests, the shared grain
corpus, the determinism guard and this log. Gates re-run after every fix.

### 1 — a proof resting on nothing reported itself closed

`Proof.closed` reduced with `all()` over its leaves, and `all(())` is `True`, so a node with
no facts and no premises asserted its conclusion out of thin air and called it proven. That
is the one answer a closed-world checker may never give, and D1 is the decision it
contradicts. Not reachable through `prove_rollup` — an empty grain is refused as
`unknown_grain` — but `closed` is the public expression of D1 and a later rule builder would
have inherited it. Now decided rather than inherited, with a control test pinning that an
R005 composition, which carries no facts of its own, stays closed through its premises.

### 2 — a comment counted four bases where the table has five

`BASIS_RULES` maps all five members of RFC 0037's closed vocabulary; the comment above it
said four. A reader checking whether the translation is complete would have counted along
with the prose and concluded one was missing.

### 3 — a docstring credited an assertion with more than it does

`test_a_superseded_rule_keeps_its_entry` claimed it "is not vacuous" while its loop runs zero
times, `SUPERSEDED` being empty. The line after the loop is what runs today; the loop is a
guard for the first split. Now said plainly, because a reader counting assertions is exactly
who that sentence misleads.

### 4 — §6's `found:` half was never populated, and no test could reach it

Patch coverage named one uncovered added line: the `found:` branch of
`Refutation.render`. The cause was not a missing test but a missing behaviour —
nothing filled the field, so every refutation stated what it required and never what
it found instead. §6's worked example is precisely a `Required:`/`Found:` pair, and
the `Found:` half is the one that tells an author which relationship to look at. A
blocked edge is exactly that "instead", and now becomes it; a refusal about the whole
question, like a refinement, still names none, and the renderer omits the label rather
than printing it with nothing after it.

Worth separating from the other three: they were prose, this was contract.

### What I would still distrust

- **`prove_rollup` is a reading, not a decision, and that is load-bearing.** Its parity with
  `can_roll_up` is asserted over the corpus's six questions and nothing wider. The two would
  drift silently on any shape the corpus does not hold, and the corpus is hand-built.
- **The rule registry has seven entries and one producer.** Every id but R006 and R007 comes
  from `BASIS_RULES`, so the "independently testable" half of D5 is asserted structurally —
  each rule has a summary and resolves — rather than by a test per rule exercising the shape
  it admits.
- **`Rule.live` and `SUPERSEDED` have no production consumer.** They are the registry surface
  D8 defines and are read only by their own test, which is the right shape for a contract
  that exists to be checked — but it does mean the append-only discipline is enforced by one
  assertion, and nothing would notice if that assertion were deleted.
- **Three of §3's seven rules are unbuilt**, now stated in D-114 rather than left to a reader
  to infer from the registry.
