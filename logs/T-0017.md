## D-088 — a dependency carries the column it was reached *through*

```divergence
decision: RFC 0037 §5.6 — the proposed FunctionalDependency IR (determinant, dependent, basis)
grade: UNLISTED
class: spec-gap
at: 2026-09-04T22:05:00Z
attempt: 2
claim: §5.6's shape cannot express §9's ambiguity refusal — a billing address and a shipping address are both `address.address_id` as a dependent and differ only in the determinant-side column that reached them, so a derivation compared on relationship names alone reports two identical routes to one entity as two meanings and two names for one relationship as ambiguity; adding `through` makes both cases correct
evidence: src/bloomery/semantic/nodes.py:161
action: decided
proposal: §5.6 gains `through: SemanticRef | None` and §9's ambiguity is defined over joined column pairs, not over relationship names
```

- **Touches:** §5.6 (illustrative, so not a lock) and §9's ambiguity refusal, which is the
  part that could not be built as written.
- **Found by a case failing, not by reading.** `order_item → customer` came back
  `AMBIGUOUS_PATH` on the corpus because `item_order` and the inverse reading of
  `order_lines` join `order_id` to `order_id` both times — one meaning, two names. A
  false refusal, and the same defect in the other direction would have been a false
  *proof*: `order → address` over `billing_address_id` and `shipping_address_id` is one
  dependent column reached two ways, and comparing dependents alone would have accepted
  it. `tests/unit/test_semantic/test_closure.py` pins both directions.
- §5.6 sketches `EntityKeyRef` and `SemanticRef` as separate types; the tree has one
  `ColumnRef`. Two dataclasses of identical fields would need converting between at every
  closure step, and the conversion is where the two readings would drift apart.

## D-091 — `CARDINALITY_EXPANDING` cannot be found by relaxing the rule

```divergence
decision: RFC 0037 §9 — refusals must distinguish a cardinality-expanding path from no functional path
grade: UNLISTED
class: discovery
at: 2026-09-04T22:40:00Z
attempt: 3
claim: the obvious implementation — re-run the closure admitting `one_to_many` in its declared direction and see whether the path completes — is near-dead code, because a `one_to_many`'s `via` lands on the target's foreign key rather than on its own key, so the target never unfolds and the relaxed closure reaches nothing; the classification is undirected connectivity over the relationship graph instead, used only after the answer is already no
evidence: src/bloomery/semantic/closure.py:351
action: decided
proposal: §9's `cardinality-expanding` is defined as "the entities are related and no admitted direction connects them", and §5.4's "must not be implemented as arbitrary graph reachability" is read as constraining acceptance, not diagnosis
```

- **Touches:** §9, and D5 (`ASSUMED`) at the edge — the row forbids *answering* the rollup
  question by reachability, which this does not do: `_connected` runs only on the refusal
  path, after the closure has already refused, and can turn one refusal into a differently
  worded refusal and nothing else.
- **The shape that survives is narrow, and it is in the suite.** A `one_to_many` whose
  `via` lands on the left entity's key makes the *right* side determine the left, so that
  is a `REFINEMENT`. What is left is a `one_to_many` joined on a column that is neither
  side's key — `test_a_path_that_only_exists_through_a_fan_out_edge_says_so`.

## D-093 — a derivation's signature has to carry the instant, not only the columns

```divergence
decision: RFC 0037 §9 — an ambiguous path is one whose alternatives imply different semantics
grade: UNLISTED
class: spec-gap
at: 2026-09-05T09:40:00Z
attempt: 1
claim: comparing routes on their joined columns alone collapses two hops that join identically and are read at different instants — a tier as of the order date and a tier as of the ship date are two numbers, and the collapse produced a RollupProof rather than an ambiguity refusal; the signature carries the anchor as well as the hop, and the hop is the whole set of column pairs rather than one of them
evidence: src/bloomery/semantic/nodes.py:206
action: decided
proposal: D11 is restated over the hop-and-instant rather than the column pair, and §9's "different semantics" is read as covering the instant a hop is read at
```

- **Reproduced before fixing.** Two `many_to_one` relationships onto one `type2` entity
  over the same key, anchored on different temporal columns:

  ```
  F2: RollupProof
      tier.band ambiguous: False [('as_ordered',)]
  ```

  and after: `RollupRefusal ambiguous_path`, both derivations held.
- The same change makes a **composite** join one reading rather than one of its columns:
  two relationships joining `(a, b)` and `(a, c)` were indistinguishable wherever `a` was
  the column the dependency happened to carry.

## Self-audit findings — 2026-09-04

Whole branch against `main`: two commits, `src/bloomery/semantic/` (4 files), the mart
guard's as-of dispatch, two import contracts, three test files, this log, and RFC 0037's
status. Departures the executor *noticed* are the fenced entries above; these are the ones
it did not, filed apart so nobody counts the two together.

Ranked by severity. All fixed on the branch.

### 1 — the blocked-edge tuple was hash-seed-ordered (D7, `LOCKED`)

`dependencies` deduplicated blocked edges through a `set` and sorted the result on
`(relationship, reason)` — a key that is not total over the node, because `BlockedEdge`
also carries the `AsOfState` that says *why*. Two edges tying on that key came out in set
iteration order, which is hash-seed order.

Not hypothetical, and not caught by any gate: the corpus the determinism guard walks had
no `one_to_one` relationship, and a `one_to_one` is the only shape where one relationship
is read in both directions and can therefore block twice in one call.

```
$ for seed in 0 1 2 3 4 5; do PYTHONHASHSEED=$seed … dependencies(p, RollupContext((('r','ordered_at'),))) …; done
['anchor_on_current', 'anchor_unknown']
['anchor_unknown', 'anchor_on_current']
['anchor_on_current', 'anchor_unknown']
['anchor_unknown', 'anchor_on_current']
['anchor_on_current', 'anchor_unknown']
['anchor_unknown', 'anchor_on_current']
```

Fixed by sorting on the node's whole value. The shape is now in the determinism guard's
corpus (`ANCHORED` in `tests/support/grain_model.py`), and narrowing the key back turns
`test_output_identical_across_hash_seeds` red.

**The rule this branch is the second instance of:** a sort key over a value that a `set`
deduplicated has to be total over that value, or the tie is decided by the hash seed. It
is the same defect as sorting quality rules without `on_fail` (RFC 0016 D50), found in a
different module two RFCs later.

### 2 — a `match` over a shared enum, ending in a catch-all

The mart guard dispatches on `AsOfState` and worded its last case under `case _`. A member
added to the shared fact later — for a validity shape nobody has built — would fall
through and be reported to a mart author with the *neighbouring* state's message: a wrong
answer, not a crash, and invisible to every existing test, because each of the four states
has a test that still passes.

`mypy --strict` does not catch it (checked: adding a member leaves the exhaustive match
clean), and `enable_error_code = ["possibly-undefined"]` fires on the *correct* code as
well, so there is no type-level gate here. Pinned instead by asserting one distinct
wording per refusing state — a new member lands on an existing message and collides.
Sabotage confirms it: adding an unhandled `AsOfState` turns three tests red.

### 3 — a dead assertion in the test for D8

`assert "grain" in flatten.lower_marts.__doc__ or True` — the `or True` makes it
unfailable, so the test pinning "the mart check has *not* moved onto the substrate" pinned
nothing. Replaced with an assertion over the names the mart module actually borrows from
`bloomery.semantic`, which is `{AsOfState, qualify_as_of}` and must stay that.

### 4 — two unreachable branches, one of them now a gate

`_admitted_directions` ended in a `return ()` after a match that covers `Cardinality`
exhaustively, and `_unknown` guarded an empty grain that `can_roll_up` has already
refused. Both removed. Dropping the first turns the exhaustiveness into a **mechanical
refusal**: adding a `Cardinality` member now fails `mypy` at the one place that has to
decide about it, which is what D-092 above says the RFC's prose cannot enforce.

```
$ uv run mypy src/bloomery/semantic   # with MANY_TO_MANY added to Cardinality
src/bloomery/semantic/closure.py:77: error: Missing return statement  [return]
```

### 5 — a docstring describing an order the code does not use

`_diagnose`'s docstring said the unanchored-historical question is "asked before either" of
the other two; the code asks refinement first. The order in the code is the right one — a
refinement is also a path through a fan-out edge, and reporting the fan-out sends the
author to correct a `cardinality:` that is already right — so the prose was corrected to
it, not the reverse.

### Also fixed

- The blocked-edge filter in `_diagnose` was written out twice; extracted.
- Four uncovered lines. Two were the dead branches above; the other two are now tested —
  a relationship read *from* a keyless entity, and `RollupContext` canonicalizing an
  unsorted argument. `src/bloomery/semantic/` is at 99.7% with no missed statements; the
  one partial branch is the implicit fallthrough of the exhaustive match, which mypy now
  proves impossible.

### What I would still distrust

- **The closure's termination is argued, not proven.** Each member holds at most two
  derivations and `_merge` keeps the two smallest by signature, so the held pair is
  monotone and the fixpoint settles — but that is an argument about a graph walk, and the
  evidence is 100 generated graphs per property (cycles included, since the strategy
  places both directions freely) plus a corpus that declares `order`↔`order_item` both
  ways. No case has failed to terminate. A malicious graph shape has not been searched for.
- **`_MAX_DERIVATIONS = 2` is a ceiling with no consumer yet.** It is safe in the
  direction that matters — a member with three routes still reports as ambiguous, so the
  cap can only cause a refusal, never a proof — but a caller that wants to *enumerate*
  routes will find two of them. RFC 0039's proof IR is where that is answered.
- **Nothing consults this package.** Every test here drives it directly, so the whole
  surface is proven against hand-built and generated IR and against nothing that a real
  spec produced. The first real fixture to reach it will be RFC 0040's, and that is where
  a mismatch between `EntityIR.key` as the grain and what a mart means by grain would
  first show up.
