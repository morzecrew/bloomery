## What the three are, and why they are one unit

Two of them close the two corpus cases that hold [RFC 0042](../rfcs/0042-semantic-bug-corpus.md)
open. `009-null-denominator` and `011-timezone-boundary` are the only cases graded
`unguarded` — bloomery compiles them and returns the wrong number — and each cites RFC 0042
D5 as the decision a converting rule answers to. `test_a_retired_rfc_owns_no_unguarded_case`
refuses a retired RFC in that position, so 0042 cannot complete until a *live* document owns
each.

They share a shape, which is why they were written together: **a fact the data does not carry
and the spec cannot state, so its absence becomes an assertion.** A wall clock with no zone
asserts UTC; a ratio with no row restriction asserts that every row belongs. Both are the
shape RFC 0061 met for currency, and both are unfalsifiable exactly where they are made.

The third is the counterpart one level up. RFC 0038 is called *"Measure semantic types and
additivity algebra"* and dimensions have no algebra at all — no rule in the registry takes a
dimension as its subject. That is a vocabulary gap rather than a wrong number, which is why
0076 is large, unscheduled, and honest about converting no case on its own.

## Self-audit

Three documents, ~900 lines, plus three index rows. Scope walked whole: every §3 claim, every
citation, every decision grade, and the two RFCs' behaviour against the fixtures they claim to
convert. Findings ranked; all fixed before this was written.

**1. R018's scope would have passed case 011 for a reason unrelated to why the case is
wrong.** The first draft fired the rule where a timestamp "becomes a time dimension". Reading
the fixture rather than its problem statement: case 011's wrong number arrives through the
metric's expression — `CASE WHEN placed_at >= TIMESTAMP '2025-02-01 …'`, a comparison against
a literal instant — and the mart's `flatten: [{date: placed_at, role: placed}]` is
*incidental*. A dimension-scoped rule fires on this fixture and would miss the same bug in a
project with no `flatten:` at all. The rule now fires where the instant's **absolute position
is consumed**, with three sites named, and D5 says which site the case actually fails at.

This is the failure where a guard passes its corpus for the wrong reason, met at design time
rather than after the sweep — and it was only visible because the fixture was read instead of
the case's prose.

**2. The new key was redundant, and would have refused the arm it claims to leave alone.**
The first draft required `zone_in:` on every parsed timestamp. Case 011's `anchored` mapping
declares its zone as `{to_utc: America/New_York}` and nothing else, so the RFC as drafted
would have refused the arm its own §4 promised would "keep compiling unchanged" — a document
contradicting itself across two sections. The fixture's own comment is the design saying it
out loud: *"`to_utc` … names the zone the wall clock was written in, which is the fact the
source system has"*. `zone_in:` now exists for the one case `to_utc` cannot express — a source
already in UTC, where `to_utc: UTC` is a no-op nobody writes — and the redundant spelling is
legal and cross-checked rather than required.

**3. A retirement SHA written from memory.** RFC 0038 was cited as retired at `2f4c5a1`; it is
`efba2b6`. Caught by checking `RETIRED.md`, which is the same way the DuckDB version was
caught two branches ago and the same failure: prose written *about* research rather than *from*
it. The fix this time is a program — see the harness below.

**4. Line citations pointed inside docstrings rather than at declarations.**
`spec/marts.py:243` is a comment line within `keep:`'s docstring; `errors.py:977` is the
remediation sentence, not `class UnsupportedHierarchy`. Both resolved to real lines, which is
why a naive check passed them, and both would send a reader to the wrong place. Corrected to
ranges that contain the declaration.

**5. An absence claim stated absolutely.** RFC 0076 §3 said *"Not one rule has a dimension as
its subject"*. True — but written from having read the list, which is indistinguishable in the
document from having assumed it. Rewritten to name what was read and what was found: a
dimension is the object of two rules (R013's dropped columns, R015's own dimension) and the
subject of none, with `closure.py:696-715` cited for why the entity-keyed machinery cannot
carry a column-to-column determination.

**6. A §3 claim nothing had run.** RFC 0075 asserted that a ratio with a filtered numerator
and an unfiltered denominator compiles today. Now measured: take case 009's `restricted` arm,
remove the `filter:` from the denominator alone, and it compiles clean with all three metrics
present. The claim was right; it was not evidence until it was run.

**7. Grade inflation, one row.** RFC 0074 D4 ("no second key restates the zone") was `LOCKED`
and is a well-argued preference rather than a decision other work rests on — the symmetric
design stays buildable. Downgraded to `ASSUMED` with the departure condition stated. Across
the three tables: 9 `LOCKED`, 7 `ASSUMED`, 7 `OPEN`.

### The harness

Finding 3 is a rule a program can enforce, so it is one. A checker walks the three documents
and verifies every `path:line` resolves and is within the file, every relative link and RFC
link resolves, every `RFC NNNN … retired at <sha>` matches `RETIRED.md`, every RFC number is
live or retired, and every short SHA is a commit in this repository.

```
uv run python <scratch>/verify_rfcs.py   ->   every citation checks out
```

It found nothing after the manual pass, which is the point: it is what makes the *next*
document's citations checkable without a manual pass. Not landed in the tree — it is scoped to
three files by name and a gate over all RFC citations is a different, larger thing worth
proposing separately.

### What was not done

- **No corpus case for RFC 0076.** §6 says a coarsening case is needed and that by RFC 0042 D4
  it belongs in that corpus first. Writing it here would put a fixture in front of the document
  that justifies it.
- **No rule implemented.** All three are design documents; R018, R019, R020 and R021 are names
  with statements and no code. `RULES` holds seventeen and the next free id is R018, checked.
- **RFC 0042's status is unchanged.** It completes when the two cases convert, which is these
  documents' execution rather than their authorship. Editing 0042's status now would claim
  something that has not happened.
- **No `pages/` change.** Each RFC's §7 names its docs and none of it ships until the design
  does.
